📐 Mathematical Foundations of Amplitude Modulation (DSB-FC)

Standard Engineering Theory

1. Baseband Message Signal m(t)

The audio studio program information (voice or music) is modeled as a sinusoidal baseband tone:

m(t) = Aₘ cos(2πfₘt)

where Aₘ is the peak audio voltage and fₘ is the message frequency (typically 300 Hz to 5 kHz in commercial broadcasting).

2. High-Frequency RF Carrier Wave c(t)

The unmodulated continuous sinusoidal carrier wave:

c(t) = A꜀ cos(2πf꜀t)

where A꜀ is the peak unmodulated carrier voltage and f꜀ is the carrier frequency (535 kHz to 1605 kHz in Medium Wave AM).

3. Conventional AM Modulated Wave s(t) & Modulation Index μ

In Double-Sideband Full-Carrier (DSB-FC) AM, the instantaneous carrier amplitude is modulated proportionally by the message signal:

s(t) = [A꜀ + m(t)] cos(2πf꜀t) = A꜀ [1 + μ cos(2πfₘt)] cos(2πf꜀t)

The Modulation Index (μ) is defined as:

μ = Aₘ / A꜀

When μ ≤ 1, the envelope [A꜀ + m(t)] never crosses zero, preserving the original audio envelope boundary without phase reversal. When μ > 1 (overmodulation), the carrier phase inverts during negative peaks, resulting in diagonal cusp distortion when demodulated by an envelope detector.

4. Spectral Expansion & Transmission Bandwidth

Applying the trigonometric product-to-sum identity reveals three distinct frequency components:

s(t) = A꜀ cos(2πf꜀t) + ½ μA꜀ cos(2π(f꜀ + fₘ)t) + ½ μA꜀ cos(2π(f꜀ - fₘ)t)

- Carrier: f꜀
- Upper Sideband (USB): f꜀ + fₘ
- Lower Sideband (LSB): f꜀ - fₘ

For single-tone sinusoidal AM, the transmission bandwidth is strictly:

BW = 2fₘ

5. Transmitter Power Distribution & Sideband Efficiency

The total broadcast power across a normalized load R = 1 Ω is:

P_total = P꜀ (1 + ½ μ²) = ½ A꜀² (1 + ½ μ²)

where P꜀ = ½ A꜀² is the unmodulated carrier power. Since the carrier transmits zero audio information, the broadcast power efficiency η is the ratio of intelligence-bearing sideband power to total power:

η = P_sidebands / P_total = μ² / (2 + μ²)

At 100% modulation (μ = 1), the maximum theoretical power efficiency is 33.3%. Two-thirds (66.7%) of broadcast transmitter power is spent purely on the unmodulated carrier.

6. Diode Envelope Detector Architecture

The greatest historical and commercial advantage of DSB-FC AM is the extreme simplicity of the receiver. A simple germanium diode followed by a parallel RC low-pass filter tracks the positive boundary of the waveform without requiring active local oscillators or microprocessors.

The RC filter time constant must satisfy the textbook condition:

1 / f꜀ ≪ RC ≪ 1 / fₘ

If RC is too small, excessive RF carrier ripple leaks through into the audio. If RC is too large, the capacitor cannot discharge quickly enough during downward message slopes, causing diagonal clipping distortion.

7. Why AM in Aviation Air Traffic Control? (No Capture Effect)

Commercial aircraft communication (118–137 MHz VHF) strictly mandates AM instead of FM. In FM receivers, the "Capture Effect" forces the detector to latch exclusively onto the stronger signal, totally silencing any weaker transmission. In AM, there is no capture effect: if two planes transmit simultaneously, both voices are heard as a heterodyned beat whistle, ensuring life-critical distress calls are never masked or silenced.
