✈️ Civil Aviation & Air Traffic Control

Why does commercial aircraft communication (118–137 MHz VHF) strictly mandate AM instead of FM? In FM, the "Capture Effect" forces the receiver to latch exclusively onto the stronger signal, totally silencing any weaker transmission. AM has no capture effect—if an emergency aircraft transmits over another tower call, both voices are heard simultaneously as a heterodyned heterodyne whistle, ensuring life-critical distress calls are never blocked.

Safety Standard: ICAO Annex 10
🌌 Long-Distance Nighttime Skywave (Ionosphere)

Medium Wave AM signals (535–1605 kHz) propagate during the daytime purely via ground waves (range ~100–200 km) because the solar-ionized D-layer absorbs RF energy. At night, the D-layer dissipates, allowing AM waves to reach the higher E and F layers of the ionosphere, reflecting back to Earth thousands of kilometers away (DX propagation).

Propagation: Ionospheric Refraction
📏 Antenna Physics & The Need for Modulation

An efficient transmission antenna requires physical height comparable to a quarter wavelength: L = λ / 4 = c / (4 × f). To transmit a 1000 Hz audio tone directly without modulation would require an antenna height of 75 kilometers! By modulating onto a 1 MHz RF carrier, the physical antenna size shrinks to an easily constructible 75 meters.

Antenna Equation: L = 300,000 / (4 × f_kHz)
📻 The Envelope Detector & Zero-Power Crystal Radios

The greatest commercial advantage of AM (DSB-FC) is the extreme simplicity of the receiver. A simple germanium diode followed by an RC low-pass filter (time constant chosen such that 1/fc ≪ RC ≪ 1/fm) recovers the original audio without needing active local oscillators or microprocessors. This enabled millions of batteryless crystal radio sets during early radio history.

Filter Criterion: 1/f_c ≪ RC ≪ 1/f_m
📐 Mathematical Derivations & Power Equations (Click to Expand)

Modulated Wave Expansion

s(t) = [Ac + Amcos(2πfmt)] cos(2πfct)

= Accos(2πfct) + ½ μ Accos(2π(fc + fm)t) + ½ μ Accos(2π(fc - fm)t)

Reveals three distinct spectral frequencies: Carrier (fc), Upper Sideband (fc+fm), and Lower Sideband (fc-fm).

Transmitter Power Distribution

Total Power: Pt = Pc (1 + μ² / 2)

Carrier Power: Pc = Ac² / (2R)

Sideband Power: Psb = Pc × (μ² / 2)

At 100% modulation (μ=1), two-thirds (66.7%) of broadcast power is consumed by the unmodulated carrier wave.

Power Efficiency & Bandwidth

Efficiency: η = Psb / Pt = μ² / (2 + μ²)

Maximum theoretical efficiency for tone AM is 33.3% at μ = 1.

Transmission Bandwidth: BW = 2 × fm

Commercial AM channels are spaced 10 kHz apart (US/FCC) or 9 kHz (Europe/Asia/ITU).
